What the literature covers, and what PreCog needs

Rows are the five pillars. Columns are the properties PreCog's architecture requires. Each cell counts the included papers whose extraction note tags that property within that pillar; click a cell to see them. Empty cells are the research gaps.

Column definitions: multi-agent state, the safety decision is taken over the joint state of several agents; non-generative inference, the decision uses a discriminative or latent predictor rather than generated rollouts or text; latency/jitter aware, inference latency or its variability is an input to, or a constraint on, the safety decision; time-to-irreversibility, the decision is made against an explicit deadline after which the hazard cannot be undone; edge deployment, the mechanism runs on the acting system's own hardware; human handoff, the mechanism passes a decision to a person and the handoff is studied; cross-domain evaluation, the same mechanism is evaluated in both a physical domain (robots, vehicles) and a logical one (software agents, transactions, services). Two robotics settings do not count as cross-domain.
