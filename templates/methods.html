PRISMA-S: how this review searches, screens and reports

Everything on this site is regenerated from three CSV files at each release, so every count can be reproduced. This page states the standards the review follows, the live PRISMA 2020 flow, and the full search protocol.

What PRISMA-S is, and why it matters here

PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses) is the reporting standard most journals require for a systematic review. The 2020 statement sets out a 27-item checklist and a flow diagram that accounts for every record from identification to inclusion, so a reader can see exactly how the evidence base was assembled and what was excluded along the way.

PRISMA-S is the 2021 extension that governs the search specifically. A search that cannot be re-run is not a systematic search, so PRISMA-S requires, for every source: the database or platform name, the exact query string, the date it was run, any filters or limits applied, and the number of records returned. It also asks for the methods used to find records outside database searching (citation chasing, hand-searching, expert contact) and for how duplicates were removed.

For a living review the standard does extra work. Each monthly run appends rows to the search log rather than replacing them, so the PRISMA-S record is a history, not a snapshot. Combined with versioned releases, that lets a paper cite one frozen state of the review, and lets a reader re-run precisely the searches that state was built on. The AI-assisted screening pass described below is declared in the same spirit: the prompt is versioned, every label is logged, and agreement with blind human screening is reported per batch.

PRISMA 2020: Page et al., BMJ 2021. PRISMA-S: Rethlefsen et al., Systematic Reviews 2021.

Standards

Review type: living systematic review. Reporting: PRISMA 2020 flow diagram and checklist; PRISMA-S for the search. Living methodology: scheduled re-searches with versioned, citable releases. Language: English. Period: 1 January 2015 onward, plus a hand-curated seed list of earlier seminal works.

Inclusion and exclusion

Include if: peer-reviewed or an arXiv preprint with a method and evaluation; English; 2015 or later (or seed list); addresses deferral or runtime safety interception, world models or latent prediction over graphs or multi-agent state, multi-agent hazard detection, edge or real-time inference under latency constraints, or human takeover and situation awareness; and reports a method, system, formal result or study rather than only a position.

Clarifications (protocol v1.2, 2026-11-09, after the first calibration round): application domain is not an inclusion criterion at any stage or pillar; a paper is judged on topic, not on whether its setting is industrial. Multi-agent coordination work (formation, consensus, path finding, coverage, swarm algorithms) is off-topic (E1) unless its contribution is a representation of shared multi-agent state that is used to evaluate safety or detect hazards, or a safety mechanism between agents. Surveys and tutorials (E3) and position papers (E2) are excluded at title and abstract and surveys are kept for snowballing.

Exclusion codes: E1 off-topic - E2 no evaluation - E3 survey or tutorial (kept for snowballing) - E4 wrong level (single-agent, single-input guardrail with no multi-agent, sequential, latency or handoff aspect) - E5 duplicate - E6 not retrievable - E7 not in English. Each exclusion records exactly one code and the counts per code are published.

Screening and AI-assistance policy

Records are deduplicated in Zotero and Rayyan, screened on title and abstract, then on full text, against the same criteria. The maintainer makes every decision.

Protocol v1.3 (2026-11-09). Title/abstract screening is AI-first with maintainer review of every inclusion. An AI model labels every record at the title/abstract stage using a fixed, versioned prompt (published in data/screening/prompts/) and gives a one-line reason. The maintainer then reads every record the AI labelled include and makes the final decision; no record enters the review without a human decision. As a leak check, a random 5% of the AI's exclusions (minimum 100 per batch, fixed seed) is also read by the maintainer; if more than 5% of that sample should have been included, the batch's exclusions are re-screened with a revised prompt. Reported per batch: records, AI includes, maintainer overrides of includes, leak-check sample size and leaks found. No AI is used at the full-text stage. Every label, decision and override is logged in decisions.csv.

Protocol v1.4 (2026-11-09): full-text stage. Records that pass title/abstract screening are first filtered by rule, each exclusion logged with its code: surveys, tutorials, magazine articles and book chapters (E3, by publication type or title; kept for snowballing); the same work published twice, such as a conference paper and its journal version (E5, later or journal version kept); and records whose full text cannot be obtained through the University of Bath library or open access (E6). The remainder is queued in three tiers by a published rule applied to the record's own title and abstract: Tier A, an explicit runtime safety mechanism between agents (collision avoidance, safety filter, barrier function, shield, runtime monitor, intervention, deferral, halt, guardrail, validator, reachability, provable safety, hazard or cascade detection) and year 2022 or later, or an LLM or tool-calling agent setting with such a mechanism; Tier B, the mechanism rule or the LLM rule alone; Tier C, the rest. Full-text review proceeds A, then B, then C, and each tier is released to the site as it completes; the PRISMA flow states how many remain. At full text an AI may draft the extraction note (criteria met with the supporting sentence, method, evaluation, proposed RQ scores, gap cells, method tags, one-line contribution) from the paper's full text; the maintainer reads the paper against the draft and makes every inclusion decision and every score. Drafts and finals are both kept.

Protocol v1.5 (2026-11-09): inclusion criteria at full text, clarified. The first full-text batches showed that two kinds of paper were competing for the same criterion. Criterion (a), runtime safety interception, is now strictly runtime: it is met only by a mechanism that sits between a nominal decision and its execution and can override, veto, halt or defer at execution time (a safety filter, shield, runtime monitor, gatekeeper or approval gate). A safety term in a training loss or a constraint inside a planner does not meet (a). Criterion (c), multi-agent hazard detection, is met when a multi-agent safety property is the paper's primary claim and its primary measured outcome (collision, violation or hazard rate against a baseline or ablation); learned safety certificates and detectors qualify, navigation and coordination planners in which collision avoidance is one constraint among several do not (E1). Every included paper is tagged runtime_interceptor yes or no, so the review can report the interceptor population separately from certificates, detectors and training-time safety. Minimum evaluation for inclusion: at least one quantitative safety metric and either a baseline or an ablation; a qualitative demonstration alone is E2. Applied from the second full-text batch; the first batch (12 records) was checked against the new wording and no decision changed.

Protocol v1.6 (2026-11-11): the evaluation floor reads against the paper's own claim. The v1.5 floor asked every included paper for a quantitative safety metric. A world model or predictor cannot report one: its result is predictive accuracy, and a hazard rate exists only for a mechanism that acts. Applied literally the floor would have excluded the representation-learning literature that pillar 2 is made of, and with it the evidence RQ1 and RQ4 rest on. The floor is therefore judged against what each paper claims. A paper claiming a safety mechanism (criteria (a) or (c)) still needs a quantitative safety metric - collision, violation, hazard or intervention rate - together with a baseline or ablation. A paper claiming a world model or predictor (criterion (b)) needs a quantitative predictive-accuracy metric together with a baseline or ablation; it enters the review as method and architecture evidence rather than safety evidence, scores no higher than 2 on RQ5, and is never counted as preventing anything. A qualitative demonstration alone remains E2 in either case. Eleven drafted records were re-tagged under this rule before any decision was taken on them; one was left excluded because its comparators are unnamed.

Protocol v1.7 (2026-11-11): the evaluation floor is a full-text criterion only. The floor was being applied at the title/abstract stage, where it does not belong: an abstract does not show a results section, so a paper whose contribution is a formal result, a verification method, a system or a qualitative study was coded E2 and dropped before anyone read it. The maintainer's blind leak check of the ACM pillar-1 run caught this. Twenty excluded records were presented with no AI label, code or reason attached; the maintainer called four of them wrongly excluded, and on re-examination three were confirmed as correct exclusions - a data-summarisation algorithm, a trust model in an iterated prisoner's dilemma, and organizational role shaping inside a training loop - while the fourth, a semantic foundation for multi-agent human cyber-physical systems, was a genuine miss. The leak rate is therefore 1 of 20, and the single leak identified a systematic coding error rather than a scope error. E2 now means no contribution at all: a position, vision or call to action, a roadmap, a keynote or invited-talk abstract, a doctoral research statement, or a work-in-progress paper reporting nothing yet. Anything contributing a method or formal result passes to full text, where the floor decides, and a record in doubt passes. The 70 E2 exclusions made under v1.5 and v1.6 were re-screened under this rule: 54 returned to full text, 9 were recoded E1 on the subject or primacy test, and 7 stood. The primacy test on criterion (c) is unchanged, and the 3,419 E1 exclusions were not revisited.

The API searches, and what has and has not been read (2026-11-12). The arXiv, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar searches for pillar 1 returned 9,187 rows, which collapse to 5,910 distinct records; 4,944 were new against everything already screened. Their title/abstract labels are the screening model's, applied under the prompt in force at the time, and the maintainer confirmed them by exception rather than record by record: a blind leak check of 182 excluded records found two wrongly excluded, both of which are now included, and the remaining labels stand as AI screening rather than as maintainer decisions. The records that passed were ranked into three tiers by how directly the title and abstract indicate a runtime safety mechanism. Tier A is being read in full. Tiers B and C are screened in but not yet read, and are reported here as such: they are counted as awaiting full text in the flow, and no paper enters the review from them until it has been read. Deduplication is counted from the records themselves rather than by subtracting known duplicates from the hit total, because a multi-database run collapses heavily before ingest and the earlier arithmetic understated duplicate removal by 3,277.

Protocol v1.8 (2026-11-12): what a first pass reads, and how accepted drafts are counted. Three things changed in practice during the first pass and are now written down. First, the tier rule orders reading and is not a relevance filter. Tier A of the API searches (513 records) was taken to full text: 458 were read, 3 are theses under embargo and are held as forthcoming, and 52 could not be retrieved or were duplicates of records already decided. To test what the unread tiers hold, 50 records were drawn at random (seed 20261112) from the 386 Tier B and C records with an arXiv copy; 49 were read and the AI draft recommended inclusion for 17 (35%), against 34% in Tier A, where the maintainer went on to accept 77% of the drafts he reviewed. The unread tiers therefore probably contain relevant papers at a rate close to Tier A, and the flow reports them as awaiting full text, not as excluded. Second, a full-text exclusion can now rest on an AI draft that the maintainer accepts without reading the paper himself. In Tier A the maintainer reviewed every drafted inclusion row by row (155 drafts: 119 kept, 36 overturned) and accepted the 303 drafted exclusions in bulk. Those are logged with no maintainer decision, counted separately in the flow, and can be reopened; no paper enters the review on an AI draft alone. Third, the reading route is recorded per decision. Where the maintainer supplied the PDF the draft quotes the paper verbatim; where the paper was read through a summarising web fetch (297 of the 458) quotes are as relayed, and values that appear only in figures or image tables were not read. Publishers that block automated access were read only when the maintainer could download them.

Snowballing as run (2026-11-12). The protocol calls for backward and forward citation chasing through Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex until a pass adds fewer than five papers. The first pass used OpenAlex alone, because Semantic Scholar throttled every request, and chased forward citations only from papers found by the database searches, capped at 100 per seed, because the hand-picked foundations are cited by whole fields outside this review. From 450 seeds it returned 11,787 records, 11,147 of them new; the screening model passed 2,518. That is far from the stopping condition, so one pass is reported and the rule is carried to the next quarterly update instead of being claimed as met. Of the 584 passed records in the top tier, 293 had a free copy the review could reach and 291 of those were read in full; the other 291 in that tier, and the 1,934 in the lower tiers, are counted as screened and not read. The maintainer reviewed all 87 drafted inclusions and kept 61. Most of what he overturned, and most of the drafted exclusions, were single-robot safety-filter papers: citation chasing follows the control-barrier-function literature well beyond multi-agent systems, which is worth knowing before the next pass.

The Tier B and C sample, as decided (2026-11-12). Of the 49 sampled records read, the AI draft recommended 17 for inclusion and the maintainer kept 10 (20%). The lower tiers are therefore not empty: on this sample roughly one record in five would be included, with wide uncertainty at this size and only for records with an arXiv copy. They remain in the flow as awaiting full text.

Second-pass title/abstract screening for records without open access (protocol v1.5, 2026-11-09). Records that passed the first title/abstract pass but whose full text is not openly available are re-screened from title and abstract under the v1.5 criteria with a second versioned prompt (ta-screen-v2), so that publisher copies are requested only where the abstract makes inclusion plausible. The same safeguards apply as in the first pass: the maintainer reviews every AI likely-include before a copy is requested, and reads a seeded random sample of the AI likely-excludes (5%, minimum 100); the sample size and leaks found are reported in the batch table. Records excluded here are counted as title/abstract exclusions in the PRISMA flow and keep both their first-pass and second-pass rows in decisions.csv. Publisher copies are obtained through the University of Bath library under its licence and are not redistributed.

Protocols v1.0 to v1.2 used blind dual-screening of a random sample with Cohen's kappa. Two calibration rounds on the first batch (IEEE Xplore, pillar 1) gave kappa of -0.09 and 0.08: the disagreements were systematic rather than random (the maintainer screened for multi-agent coordination; the written criteria and the AI screened for safety mechanisms between agents) and blind screening at 3,759 records per database was not sustainable for a single maintainer. Both rounds are kept in decisions.csv as calibration data. The review therefore moved to AI-first screening with full human review of inclusions, which keeps every inclusion a human decision while making the workload proportional to the literature that matters.
