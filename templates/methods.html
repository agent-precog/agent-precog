PRISMA-S: how this review searches, screens and reports

Everything on this site is regenerated from three CSV files at each release, so every count can be reproduced. This page states the standards the review follows, the live PRISMA 2020 flow, and the full search protocol.

What PRISMA-S is, and why it matters here

PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses) is the reporting standard most journals require for a systematic review. The 2020 statement sets out a 27-item checklist and a flow diagram that accounts for every record from identification to inclusion, so a reader can see exactly how the evidence base was assembled and what was excluded along the way.

PRISMA-S is the 2021 extension that governs the search specifically. A search that cannot be re-run is not a systematic search, so PRISMA-S requires, for every source: the database or platform name, the exact query string, the date it was run, any filters or limits applied, and the number of records returned. It also asks for the methods used to find records outside database searching (citation chasing, hand-searching, expert contact) and for how duplicates were removed.

For a living review the standard does extra work. Each monthly run appends rows to the search log rather than replacing them, so the PRISMA-S record is a history, not a snapshot. Combined with versioned releases, that lets a paper cite one frozen state of the review, and lets a reader re-run precisely the searches that state was built on. The AI-assisted screening pass described below is declared in the same spirit: the prompt is versioned, every label is logged, and agreement with blind human screening is reported per batch.

PRISMA 2020: Page et al., BMJ 2021. PRISMA-S: Rethlefsen et al., Systematic Reviews 2021.

Standards

Review type: living systematic review. Reporting: PRISMA 2020 flow diagram and checklist; PRISMA-S for the search. Living methodology: scheduled re-searches with versioned, citable releases. Language: English. Period: 1 January 2015 onward, plus a hand-curated seed list of earlier seminal works.

Inclusion and exclusion

Include if: peer-reviewed or an arXiv preprint with a method and evaluation; English; 2015 or later (or seed list); addresses deferral or runtime safety interception, world models or latent prediction over graphs or multi-agent state, multi-agent hazard detection, edge or real-time inference under latency constraints, or human takeover and situation awareness; and reports a method, system, formal result or study rather than only a position.

Clarifications (protocol v1.2, 2026-11-09, after the first calibration round): application domain is not an inclusion criterion at any stage or pillar; a paper is judged on topic, not on whether its setting is industrial. Multi-agent coordination work (formation, consensus, path finding, coverage, swarm algorithms) is off-topic (E1) unless its contribution is a representation of shared multi-agent state that is used to evaluate safety or detect hazards, or a safety mechanism between agents. Surveys and tutorials (E3) and position papers (E2) are excluded at title and abstract and surveys are kept for snowballing.

Exclusion codes: E1 off-topic - E2 no evaluation - E3 survey or tutorial (kept for snowballing) - E4 wrong level (single-agent, single-input guardrail with no multi-agent, sequential, latency or handoff aspect) - E5 duplicate - E6 not retrievable - E7 not in English. Each exclusion records exactly one code and the counts per code are published.

Screening and AI-assistance policy

Records are deduplicated in Zotero and Rayyan, screened on title and abstract, then on full text, against the same criteria. The maintainer makes every decision.

Protocol v1.3 (2026-11-09). Title/abstract screening is AI-first with maintainer review of every inclusion. An AI model labels every record at the title/abstract stage using a fixed, versioned prompt (published in data/screening/prompts/) and gives a one-line reason. The maintainer then reads every record the AI labelled include and makes the final decision; no record enters the review without a human decision. As a leak check, a random 5% of the AI's exclusions (minimum 100 per batch, fixed seed) is also read by the maintainer; if more than 5% of that sample should have been included, the batch's exclusions are re-screened with a revised prompt. Reported per batch: records, AI includes, maintainer overrides of includes, leak-check sample size and leaks found. No AI is used at the full-text stage. Every label, decision and override is logged in decisions.csv.

Protocols v1.0 to v1.2 used blind dual-screening of a random sample with Cohen's kappa. Two calibration rounds on the first batch (IEEE Xplore, pillar 1) gave kappa of -0.09 and 0.08: the disagreements were systematic rather than random (the maintainer screened for multi-agent coordination; the written criteria and the AI screened for safety mechanisms between agents) and blind screening at 3,759 records per database was not sustainable for a single maintainer. Both rounds are kept in decisions.csv as calibration data. The review therefore moved to AI-first screening with full human review of inclusions, which keeps every inclusion a human decision while making the workload proportional to the literature that matters.
