# ta-screen-v4, Pillar 4 scope addendum (maintainer rulings, 2026-11-13)

Given to the screening model together with ta-screen-v4 for every Pillar 4 record. It was reached in three steps, all on 2026-11-13, and the labels from each step are in decisions.csv:

1. A first screen of the IEEE Xplore and ACM records under a loose reading of criterion (d) (an explicit latency constraint plus a safety-relevant decision) passed 173 of 417, most of them application papers that only report speed on edge hardware.
2. The maintainer ruled that timing must be the contribution. The 173 were re-screened and the Scopus records screened under that rule, which also required a safety-relevant setting: 61 of 928 passed.
3. The maintainer then ruled that a safety-relevant application is not required. Every record excluded as off-topic was re-screened under the text below, which is the rule in force.

- These records come from the Pillar 4 search (edge inference and real-time constraints). The maintainer's scope ruling of 2026-11-13 applies and overrides a looser reading of criterion (d). A record passes under (d) ONLY IF BOTH hold:
  (1) TIMING IS THE CONTRIBUTION. Meeting, guaranteeing, predicting or analysing inference timing is what the paper is about: deadline-aware or deadline-constrained inference or scheduling of learned models; timing-predictable or worst-case-bounded inference; tail latency, p99 or jitter analysis of inference or of the network path that carries it; accuracy explicitly traded against a stated latency budget or deadline (anytime, early-exit, adaptive or budgeted inference); or a study of how latency, jitter or wireless delay degrades a time-critical decision.
  (2) LEARNED-MODEL INFERENCE. The timing concerns inference with a learned model (a neural network or other trained model) at the edge, on a device, or split between device and edge. By the maintainer's ruling of 2026-11-13 (second part), a SAFETY-RELEVANT APPLICATION IS NOT REQUIRED: a paper whose contribution is deadline-constrained, latency-budgeted, timing-predictable or tail-latency-aware inference passes even when the application is generic or safety is mentioned only as motivation.
- Exclude E1 (off-topic for this pillar): an APPLICATION paper that builds or deploys a detector, classifier, segmenter or controller on a Jetson or other edge device and merely REPORTS its speed (frames per second, milliseconds per frame, "real-time") without a stated deadline or budget that the work is designed around; model compression, pruning or quantisation reported as speed and accuracy only; hardware accelerators or SoCs with no timing guarantee as the subject; on-device or federated training; generic offloading or resource scheduling with no learned safety decision; classical (non-learned) pipelines.
- When in doubt between "timing is the contribution" and "timing is a reported number", look at what the title and the first and last sentences of the abstract claim as the contribution. If the claim is a new detector or application and latency is one result among several, exclude E1. If the claim is about deadlines, latency budgets, predictability, tail latency or jitter, include.
- Use E3 for surveys and E2 or E4 only as the prompt defines them. Criteria (a), (b), (c) and (e) still apply if a record clearly meets one of them.
