# ta-screen-v4, Pillar 4 scope addendum (maintainer ruling, 2026-11-13)

Given to the screening model together with ta-screen-v4 for every Pillar 4 record. A first screen of the IEEE Xplore and ACM records under a looser reading of criterion (d) (an explicit latency constraint plus a safety-relevant decision) passed 173 of 417, most of them application papers that only report speed on edge hardware. The maintainer ruled that timing must be the contribution; those 173 were re-screened under the text below, which was then used for all further Pillar 4 records. Both sets of labels are in decisions.csv.

- These records come from the Pillar 4 search (edge inference and real-time constraints). The maintainer's scope ruling of 2026-11-13 applies and overrides a looser reading of criterion (d). A record passes under (d) ONLY IF BOTH hold:
  (1) TIMING IS THE CONTRIBUTION. Meeting, guaranteeing, predicting or analysing inference timing is what the paper is about: deadline-aware or deadline-constrained inference or scheduling of learned models; timing-predictable or worst-case-bounded inference; tail latency, p99 or jitter analysis of inference or of the network path that carries it; accuracy explicitly traded against a stated latency budget or deadline (anytime, early-exit, adaptive or budgeted inference); or a study of how latency, jitter or wireless delay degrades a time-critical decision.
  (2) SAFETY-RELEVANT USE. The inference serves a safety-critical or time-critical decision or system (autonomous driving or flight, robot or vehicle control, collision avoidance, hazard or fault response, medical alarms, multi-agent or multi-robot coordination), stated as the setting of the work and not merely as motivation in one sentence.
- Exclude E1 (off-topic for this pillar): an APPLICATION paper that builds or deploys a detector, classifier, segmenter or controller on a Jetson or other edge device and merely REPORTS its speed (frames per second, milliseconds per frame, "real-time") without a stated deadline or budget that the work is designed around; model compression, pruning or quantisation reported as speed and accuracy only; hardware accelerators or SoCs with no timing guarantee as the subject; edge or on-device inference with no safety use; on-device or federated training; generic offloading or resource scheduling with no learned safety decision; classical (non-learned) pipelines.
- When in doubt between "timing is the contribution" and "timing is a reported number", look at what the title and the first and last sentences of the abstract claim as the contribution. If the claim is a new detector or application and latency is one result among several, exclude E1. If the claim is about deadlines, latency budgets, predictability, tail latency or jitter, include.
- Use E3 for surveys and E2 or E4 only as the prompt defines them. Criteria (a), (b), (c) and (e) still apply if a record clearly meets one of them.
