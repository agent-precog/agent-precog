# ta-screen-v4, Pillar 5 scope addendum (maintainer ruling, 2026-11-13)

Applied on top of ta-screen-v4 to every Pillar 5 record. Pillar 5 is human-automation interaction: what a human supervisor needs in order to take over from automation, how automation erodes that capacity, and who carries responsibility when it fails. Criterion (e) is the relevant one. The boundary with Pillar 1 was settled by the maintainer on 2026-11-13 and is not retrospective: the 27 takeover and situation-awareness records restored to Pillar 1 earlier that day stay there. Text given to the screening model:

- These records come from the Pillar 5 search. Criterion (e) is "human takeover, handoff, or situation awareness when automation defers to a person". Label include where the paper's subject is one of: transfer of control from automation to a person (takeover requests, takeover time, takeover quality or readiness); the out-of-the-loop performance decrement; automation complacency, over-trust, under-trust or trust calibration; mode confusion and automation surprise; an operator's or supervisor's situation awareness, workload or vigilance while monitoring automation; transparency, explanation or interface design intended to support a supervisor's awareness or takeover; or responsibility, blame and moral crumple zones when automation fails.
- BOUNDARY WITH PILLAR 1. Pillar 1 keeps criterion (e) records where the hand-off is part of a runtime safety mechanism in a multi-agent or AI system, such as a shield that escalates to a human or a monitor that alerts the supervisor of a robot fleet. Pillar 5 takes records about the human's capacity, awareness, trust and responsibility. Where a record could sit in either, judge by the measured outcome: a human measure (takeover time, situation-awareness score, trust rating, workload, error rate) is Pillar 5; a system measure (hazard rate, collision rate, intervention or violation rate) is Pillar 1. Say which pillar in the reason.
- Exclude E1 where "takeover" or "handover" is used in another sense entirely: corporate takeover, cellular or network handover, robot-to-human object handover studied as grasping, or patient handover in clinical communication with no automation. Also E1 for autonomy, control or teleoperation work in which the human appears only as a data source, a modelled disturbance or an operator of conventional equipment, with no measure of awareness, trust, workload or transfer of control; and for driver state or drowsiness detection unless it is tied to takeover readiness or situation awareness.
- A record may be in scope even where the automation is not learned or AI: this pillar does not carry the Pillar 1 learned-system ruling.
- Use E2, E3 and E7 as the prompt defines them. Surveys of the human-factors literature are E3 and are kept for reference.
- An empty or missing abstract is not by itself a reason to exclude. Judge on the title.
